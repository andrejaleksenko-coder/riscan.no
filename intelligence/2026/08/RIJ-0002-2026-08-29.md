Executive Brief
The structural thesis recorded in RIJ-0001 remains valid.
AI infrastructure continues to dominate the global memory market, but the situation is becoming more pronounced.
DRAM supply remains tight. HBM demand continues to compete directly with conventional DRAM for manufacturing capacity, while cloud service providers continue increasing infrastructure expenditure.
TrendForce now estimates that DRAM and NAND Flash together may account for 47% of major cloud service providers' capital expenditure in 2026 and as much as 68% in 2027. This indicates that memory itself is becoming one of the central economic constraints in AI infrastructure deployment.
At the same time, Chinese memory manufacturers are becoming increasingly important. CXMT is advancing technologically in DRAM, while YMTC is pursuing aggressive expansion in NAND.
Geopolitical risk has also increased significantly since RIJ-0001. Shipping through the Strait of Hormuz remains constrained amid conflict involving Iran and the United States, creating additional energy, freight and inflation risk.
For RISCAN, the strategic conclusion remains unchanged:
Do not speculate on inventory. Build intelligence first.
During this review period RISCAN also moved from theoretical supplier diversification toward an actual supplier-data architecture.
The first supplier selected for integration is ALSO Germany.
Geopolitics
United States / China
The semiconductor industry continues to move away from unrestricted globalization toward strategically controlled supply chains.
Memory technology, AI accelerators, semiconductor manufacturing equipment and advanced packaging are increasingly treated as national infrastructure.
The consequences for RISCAN are not limited to direct import restrictions.
Political intervention can influence:
manufacturing capacity,
technology availability,
supplier geography,
shipping routes,
currency exposure,
and ultimately European wholesale prices.
Therefore country-of-origin and geopolitical exposure should eventually become measurable attributes in RISCAN supplier intelligence.
China
The importance of Chinese memory manufacturing increased further during the review period.
CXMT continues closing the technological gap in DRAM. On 29 August, Reuters reported that CXMT is supplying its latest LPDDR6 memory for Xiaomi's upcoming flagship foldable smartphone, demonstrating increasingly advanced commercial deployment rather than purely experimental production.
YMTC is simultaneously pursuing aggressive NAND expansion.
Recent reporting indicates that YMTC aims to challenge the established global NAND leaders and has sought substantial new capital for production upgrades and research.
For RISCAN this strengthens the conclusion from RIJ-0001:
Chinese memory manufacturers cannot be ignored.
However, potential sourcing opportunities must always be evaluated together with export-control, sanctions and long-term supply-chain risks.
Middle East / Shipping
Logistics risk has increased materially since RIJ-0001.
Shipping through the Strait of Hormuz remains constrained amid continuing geopolitical conflict.
Reuters reported on 27 August that visible commodity-vessel transits remained below their recent moving average. Traffic around Bab el-Mandeb had also slowed.
On 28 August, Iran stated that it maintained control over the Strait of Hormuz, illustrating the continuing political and security uncertainty surrounding the route.
The immediate RISCAN supply chain is primarily European.
However, many products sold by European distributors originate in:
South Korea,
Taiwan,
China,
Japan,
Malaysia,
and other Asian manufacturing locations.
Therefore disruption between Asia and Europe can eventually appear inside European distributor pricing even when RISCAN itself purchases from Germany or another EU country.
SSD / NAND Market
The NAND market remains strongly influenced by AI infrastructure and enterprise storage.
The longer-term NAND outlook, however, appears less structurally constrained than DRAM.
Manufacturers continue investing in higher-density NAND and enterprise SSD capacity, while weaker consumer demand can periodically create different pricing conditions between enterprise and consumer storage.
This divergence is strategically important for RISCAN.
RISCAN does not need to predict the absolute bottom of the NAND market.
Instead, the system should detect temporary purchasing windows when European distributor prices for high-quality consumer SSDs decline relative to the broader memory market.
The primary products to monitor remain:
Samsung,
Crucial / Micron,
Kingston,
WD / SanDisk,
SK hynix / Solidigm.
YMTC should remain under observation as a potential future supplier/manufacturer relationship rather than an immediate primary sourcing channel.
RAM / DRAM Market
The DRAM thesis from RIJ-0001 has strengthened.
TrendForce reported during August that consumer DRAM remains undersupplied and contract prices continue rising, although the rate of increase may slow as buyers become increasingly resistant to higher costs.
On 19 August, TrendForce reported that DRAM contract prices remained firm while spot-market activity remained subdued. Manufacturers are expanding capacity, but those expansions will occur over multiple years rather than immediately.
The most important structural factor remains HBM.
TrendForce's August HBM analysis states that HBM demand will continue crowding out conventional DRAM supply into 2027. Negotiations for 2027 HBM supply remain difficult as manufacturers seek higher prices.
This means that ordinary workstation and consumer memory is increasingly connected economically to the AI infrastructure market.
RISCAN should therefore avoid treating consumer RAM as an isolated retail category.
Its pricing intelligence must include the wider DRAM/HBM market.
AI & Data Centers
AI infrastructure remains the dominant structural force affecting RISCAN's future hardware market.
The scale of memory expenditure is becoming particularly important.
TrendForce estimates that DRAM and NAND Flash could represent approximately 68% of major cloud service providers' capital expenditure in 2027, compared with 47% in 2026.
This suggests a fundamental change in the economics of AI infrastructure.
Compute availability alone is no longer the only constraint.
Memory capacity, memory bandwidth and storage infrastructure are becoming strategic resources themselves.
The effect extends beyond HBM.
AI servers require:
HBM,
server DRAM,
enterprise SSDs,
networking,
power infrastructure,
and increasingly large storage systems.
Therefore RISCAN's future intelligence layer should not monitor SSD and RAM prices independently.
It should monitor the entire AI infrastructure cycle that drives those prices.
Manufacturing Capacity
Manufacturers are responding to shortages with significant investment.
However, RIJ-0001 correctly noted that new semiconductor capacity requires years rather than months.
A clear example appeared during this review period.
On 27 August, SK hynix held the groundbreaking ceremony for its first U.S. AI-memory production facility in Indiana.
The facility is intended to produce next-generation HBM and advanced packaging, but production is targeted for 2029.
This demonstrates an important market principle:
Investment announcement does not equal immediate supply.
Billions of dollars can be committed today while current market shortages continue for several years.
RISCAN therefore should not interpret large semiconductor investment announcements as immediate signals to expect falling prices.

This maps directly onto the RISCAN architecture.
Example:

ALSO Bestand
        ↓

This is strategically important.
RISCAN will not need to identify products by unreliable text matching when structured manufacturer identifiers are available.

Supplier architecture
RISCAN now separates three concepts:

    ↓
What the product IS

suppliers
    ↓
Who can supply it

    ↓
At what price and availability
This distinction is fundamental.
One Samsung SSD can eventually have offers from:
ALSO,
TD SYNNEX,
Ingram Micro,
and other distributors.
RISCAN can then compare the offers without creating duplicate products.
RISCAN Intelligence Infrastructure
During this review period the internal product architecture advanced significantly.
RISCAN's product catalogue is now connected to SQLite rather than being dependent on a hard-coded JavaScript product array.
The first product can be retrieved through the application API from the database.
A flexible product_specs architecture has also been introduced.
Instead of adding dozens of category-specific columns to the main products table, specifications can be stored separately.
Example:
Samsung 990 PRO 2TB

capacity     → 2TB
interface    → PCIe 4.0 NVMe
form_factor  → M.2 2280
Inventory has also been separated conceptually into:
stock_physical
stock_supplier
stock_incoming
This distinction will eventually allow RISCAN to tell customers whether a product:
is physically held by RISCAN,
is available from a distributor,
or is already ordered and in transit.
The first supplier collector module has also been created for ALSO Germany.
Its current role is deliberately limited.
It can identify the supplier and match the Samsung reference product using its MPN.
Live supplier pricing has not yet been inserted.
This follows a core RISCAN intelligence rule established during this period:
Unknown data is better than false data.
No artificial supplier price will be inserted merely to make the system appear complete.
Norway / Import Environment
The fundamental Norwegian import model remains unchanged from RIJ-0001.
For RISCAN, the important economic variables are not simply distributor purchase price.
The future landed-cost engine must consider:
supplier purchase price
+ freight
+ currency conversion
+ payment/transaction cost
+ import-related processing
+ VAT accounting treatment
+ RISCAN operating margin
As the supplier database develops, RISCAN should therefore avoid comparing suppliers purely by displayed EUR purchase price.
A cheaper distributor can become the more expensive supplier after logistics and other costs are included.
