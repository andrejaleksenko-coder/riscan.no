## Executive Brief

Memory remains in a structurally tight market driven primarily by AI
infrastructure.

DRAM and NAND demand from AI servers and data centers continues to absorb
production capacity that would otherwise serve consumer markets.

At the same time, geopolitical intervention in semiconductor supply chains
is increasing.

For RISCAN, the current environment favors diversification of suppliers,
careful inventory management and continuous price monitoring rather than
large speculative purchases.

---

## Geopolitics

### United States / China

The United States announced new trade measures covering polysilicon and
related products used in semiconductor and solar manufacturing.

A 15% tariff and minimum import prices are planned, with implementation
scheduled for December 2026.

This is another indication that semiconductor supply chains are increasingly
being treated as strategic infrastructure rather than ordinary global trade.

### China

Chinese memory manufacturers CXMT and YMTC continue expanding their influence.

CXMT has become an increasingly important DRAM producer while YMTC remains
a significant NAND manufacturer.

Their expansion creates potential future sourcing opportunities, but also
introduces geopolitical and export-control risks.

### Shipping

Security risks remain elevated around the Red Sea and the Strait of Hormuz.

For RISCAN this matters because disruption of Asian-European shipping routes
can increase freight costs and delivery times.

---

## SSD / NAND Market

AI infrastructure remains the principal growth engine for NAND.

Enterprise SSD demand is particularly strong while consumer electronics
demand remains considerably weaker.

Manufacturers continue reallocating advanced production capacity toward
higher-margin enterprise and data-center products.

NAND supply is expected to remain constrained through much of 2026.

Market conditions may begin moving toward better balance during the second
half of 2027 if planned production improvements materialize.

Samsung has introduced V10 Bonding V-NAND with more than 400 layers,
demonstrating that NAND development is increasingly being driven by
AI-scale storage requirements.

---

## RAM / DRAM Market

DRAM remains under strong supply pressure.

AI servers and HBM production continue consuming manufacturing capacity.

Server DRAM contract prices are expected to continue increasing during Q3 2026.

Long-term supply agreements between large cloud providers and memory
manufacturers are becoming increasingly important.

This creates a disadvantage for smaller buyers who do not have guaranteed
allocation contracts.

---

## AI & Data Centers

AI infrastructure remains the dominant structural force in the memory market.

Demand is moving beyond AI training toward inference.

Inference requires not only accelerators and HBM but increasingly large
amounts of conventional DRAM and high-performance storage.

This means AI demand now affects products much closer to RISCAN's future
consumer and workstation market.

---

## Manufacturing Capacity

SK hynix approved approximately $38 billion of new semiconductor manufacturing
investment in South Korea through 2031.

The investment includes advanced DRAM/HBM production and new NAND capacity.

However, significant new capacity requires years to become operational.

Therefore investment announcements today do not immediately solve current
supply shortages.

---

## Norway / Import Environment

Electrical and electronic products generally have no Norwegian customs duty,
but Norwegian VAT applies.

For VAT-registered businesses, import VAT is reported through the VAT return.

Logistics, currency exchange rates and customs-processing costs therefore
remain important components of RISCAN's landed cost calculation.

## Impact on RISCAN

### Short term — 0–3 months

Do not build large speculative inventories.

Monitor retail and distributor prices frequently.

Begin identifying multiple European suppliers for identical SKUs.

Track Crucial, Samsung, Kingston, WD/Sandisk and SK hynix/Solidigm products.

---

### Medium term — 3–12 months

Develop direct relationships with distributors and manufacturers.

Monitor CXMT and YMTC development, but treat geopolitical exposure as a
separate sourcing risk.

Build automated historical price tracking.

Introduce supplier reliability scoring into the RISCAN database.

---

### Long term — 1–3 years

RISCAN should avoid becoming dependent on any single country,
manufacturer or distributor.

The strongest position will be a diversified supplier network connecting:

Europe
United States
South Korea
Taiwan
China
Japan

The competitive advantage should not simply be the lowest price.

It should be:

reliable sourcing,
transparent pricing,
realistic delivery times,
verified hardware,
and trustworthy market intelligence.

## CTO Forecast

### Forecast 1 — Memory prices

Memory prices are likely to remain structurally elevated and volatile
through the remainder of 2026.

**Confidence:** 85%

Reason:
AI infrastructure continues absorbing DRAM and NAND capacity while new
manufacturing capacity cannot appear quickly.

---

### Forecast 2 — Consumer SSD opportunity

Consumer SSD pricing may become less aggressive than enterprise pricing
because consumer demand remains weaker.

This may periodically create purchasing windows for RISCAN.

**Confidence:** 70%

---

### Forecast 3 — China

Chinese memory manufacturers will become increasingly important to the
global market during the next several years.

However, geopolitical restrictions may limit where their products can be
sold or incorporated.

**Confidence:** 80%

---

### Forecast 4 — RISCAN strategy

Supplier diversification will become more valuable than attempting to predict
the absolute lowest market price.

**Confidence:** 90%
